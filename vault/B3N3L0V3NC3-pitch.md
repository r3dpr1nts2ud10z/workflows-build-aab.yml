## LOGLINE
When two visionary architects from Brooklyn and the Bronx accidentally create a hyperintelligent fox in a blackout, their stolen inventions become the battleground of a sixty-year conspiracy to erase New York City's future — one generation at a time. The fox's only way to stop it: live through every era the machine ever touched.

## THE ONE-LINER UNDER THE LOGLINE
*Who Framed Roger Rabbit* × *Samurai Champloo* × *Spawn* × *Batman Beyond* — set across sixty years of real NYC history, scored like a mixtape.

---

## THE WORLD: R3D NYC
New York City — but the city has always been a civilization of humans, muppets, and talking humanlike animals, in every era, one ledger. Never explained, never winked at. The mixed city is the show's physics and its politics: every lineage, every species, one set of jokes (the anybody-can-get-roasted law) and one set of debts.

The eras run **parallel and forward-adjacent** — 1964, 1977, 1985, the '90s, 2003, 2008, the audit era, and the present are all live at once. Which means a murder in the past isn't a murder — it's a **genocide of descent**: every child, movement, and block that would have grown from that life un-happens. The villain's endgame weaponizes this. The show's finale answers it.

## THE PREMISE
**2003. The second blackout.** Lorenzo Nitti (Brooklyn, Pace University) and Xadel Bambino (the Bronx, BMCC) — two rare multiracial architects (Black, Italian, Blackfoot) whose schools were shuttered — build two world-changing machines out of a Jersey City incubator pod:
- **R3DPR1NT** — teleportation across the timeline
- **N3PSQAL3** — a revival-and-restoration engine ("the humanifier")

At the City Council Innovations Convention, one machine is anointed and one is buried — through a gate that was never neutral. **The Corporal**, a lawman-turned-machine who has run the same scheme through six decades of NYC history, illegally screens the future and plants a mole: **Ramon**, an orphan his own machinery created, who befriends the architects two weeks before the Convention.

The day after the fork, the lights die. In the darkness, an injured fox in Xadel's healing chamber has its dials accidentally set to maximum — and steps out standing 5'7", with Bruce Lee's mind, a hypermillennial mouth, and no life experience whatsoever.

His name is **Ph03x**. He is the miracle the machine accidentally birthed — and the machine's hunt-dog is already in the room.

## THE ENGINE
Ph03x was crafted with all the knowledge and none of the living. Nitti's secret fail-safe protocol routes him **through the eras themselves** — 1964's promise, 1977's blackout, the '90s golden age, 2008's collapse, the audit years — to earn the life experience he was built without. Every episode is a decade lap: an AI becoming human, learning its soul's origin source. The curriculum is also a case file — the eras trace the Corporal's sixty-year scheme, and the student is unknowingly studying his enemy.

## THE ANTAGONISTS (the register laws)
- **The Corporal** — ARGUABLE, always. Magneto's polarity with a Joker/Two-Face infusion: a villain whose case the audience can't fully dismiss, running a citywide expiration date only the elite can see.
- **Ramon** — TRAGIC, always. The Sentinel with a childhood wound: orphaned by the machine, weaponized by it, aimed at the one being who shares his coordinates.
- **Ph03x** — LOVABLE-FIRST. The legend earns the gravitas late.

## THE ENDGAME (season architecture)
The present day is revealed to be **the R3DGAT0RY** — the purgatory where sixty years of erased souls wait for settlement. The finale delivers: a fallen hero, a fusion foretold (**the Ph03n1x** — Father Time and Mother Nature in one body), a boss fight scored by a unified acapella started by a homeless muppet, and an ending where the restored souls of every era **amend their own justice** — the heroes retrieve and restore, but never execute. Fate is what's done to you; destiny is what's settled for you.

## THE SOUND
The show scores itself like a blend tape — eras layered over eras, exactly like its timeline physics. Signature sequence: the climactic battle is fourth-wall interrupted by three real legendary NYC DJs (DooWop, Kid Capri, Ron G) flipping the fight's anthem across three classic instrumentals in a live mixtape relay — the world visualizing its own culture source in real time.

## THE COMEDY
Distributed, not parked: every 45-minute slot runs ~25 minutes of plot with ~20 minutes of hijinks threaded between the movements. Cold open of the pilot: a mother spazzing on a whole Brooklyn townhouse, a transit odyssey across three boros, and an AI-motion-imposed recreation of a real battle-rap classic at the PATH station stairs. The funniest scenes are carrying the sharpest daggers.

## PILOT (EPISODE 1) — "the tracks and the train"
Strictly human, no fox. One night and one day: the trio celebrates their Convention acceptances across a three-boro transit odyssey (Brooklyn J train → Gray's Papaya → PATH → Jersey City), the Convention forks two best friends' futures through a rigged gate, and the lights die on the closing frame. The audience meets the knife as a friend, parties in an apartment they won't know is a crime scene for a full season, and laughs at an old man in novelty shades who is secretly the saddest plant in the show. Episode 2 opens the chamber.

## FRANCHISE ARCHITECTURE
B3N3L0V3NC3 is the narrative front door of a real product line: **R3DPR1NT, the Civilization Simulation Game**, and **N3PSQAL3, the Clearinghouse Protocol** — both in development at r3dpr1nt S2UD10Z. The audience watches the architects build what the studio ships. The show sells the world; the world feeds the products.
